## What the 2026 model did to the inconsistencies found in 2025

`src/inconsistencies.qmd` records 31 problems found while translating the 2025
model. This is what became of each of them in 2026. It was checked by hand,
variable by variable, against the two Vensim sources.

Four outcomes, and the distinction matters:

| | |
|---|---|
| **dissolved** | the construct causing it was removed. The problem cannot recur, and the R translating it is dead code |
| **still there** | the equation is unchanged, or changed without touching the problem. Yours to fix, as before |
| **changed** | the equation moved and the problem may or may not survive. Needs reading |
| **not Vensim's** | it was an error in the R translation, or an economic question. 2026 says nothing about it either way |

---

### Dissolved — the construct is gone

| Issue | What happened |
|---|---|
| `Tax progressivity multiplier` — the factor rises, falls, then rises again | `Tax progressivity multiplier 2..6` removed with the progressive income tax policy |
| `Act_BI and Act_progrIncomeTax` | both switches removed |
| `SH_renew_i` | `RES share i` removed |
| `F_govWageCompWTR`, `level WTR gov paid gis` | the whole work-time-reduction policy removed |
| `R_gWageCompWTR_i` — `delta wage WTR` | removed |
| `R_hrWage_isg` vs min and max wage | `MIN wage gis` and `MAX wage gis` removed with the wage policies |
| `R_E_s and R_E_sg` — **the Job Guarantee subtracted twice** | see below: this one is worth reading |
| `L T gisv and L gis` — `male share delay is` where `male share is` was meant | `L gis` and `L is` removed; the 2026 model has `Ld gis` and `Ld i s` instead |

**The double subtraction is the clearest case.** In 2025 the Job Guarantee
appeared in both halves of the unemployment rate:

```
u gs = max(0.01, (labour supply gs - sum(L gis) - JG L gs) / (labour supply gs + JG L gs))
```

subtracted from the numerator *and* added to the denominator. In 2026:

```
u gs = max(0.01, (labour supply gs - sum(Ld gis)) / labour supply gs)
```

`JG L gs` is gone entirely, with the Job Guarantee module. The problem is not
fixed — it is dissolved, which is a different thing: if the policy ever comes
back, so does the question.

Note what did **not** go: the `max(0.01, ...)` floor is still there. That is the
separate issue recorded in `src/assumptions.qmd` as *"Exogenous minimum amount
of (frictional) unemployment — THIS IS ALMOST AN INCONSISTENCY"*. Still true.

---

### Still there — unchanged, or changed without addressing it

| Issue | 2026 |
|---|---|
| `taxable inc u gs` — pensioners charged employee social contributions | **equation identical.** `gross annual income u gs * (1 - ss rate employee)` in both |
| **Covid, the asymmetry between the three shocks** | **identical.** `c19 shock exp` still stops at `Time=11`; `c19 shock cons` and `c19 shock inv` still run to `Time=12`. Exports get two Covid years, consumption and investment three, and nothing says why |
| `SH_skill_is` — initial shares summing to slightly more than 1 | **the data is byte-identical.** `skill distribution initial is` has the same values; only the `/ Year 1` was dropped from the `INTEG` |
| `R_u_sg` — negative initial values for males | `u gs` was rewritten, but the `max(0.01, ...)` floor is what masks it, and that is unchanged |

The Covid one is the one to look at first. It is small, it is certainly
unintentional, and it is still there.
